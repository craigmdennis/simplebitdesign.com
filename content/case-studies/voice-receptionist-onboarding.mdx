## Every new customer needed a human to get started

Setting up the AI voice receptionist meant a person walking each customer through configuration. That capped how fast the product could grow, and it meant the first experience of an AI product was a human doing the hard part. New customers couldn't tell what the AI would do on their behalf, so they couldn't trust it before it was already live and answering their calls.

## The bottleneck wasn't onboarding capacity. It was the configuration model.

The obvious fix was to hire more people to run more onboardings. That scales cost, not the product. The real problem was that configuration was built to be operated by an expert, not understood by a customer. To grow without a human in every setup, the customer had to be able to configure the AI themselves and believe it was set up correctly.

## Defaults that worked out of the box, with control that appeared as needs grew

I redesigned the configuration experience around sensible out-of-the-box defaults, so a new customer got a working AI receptionist immediately, then exposed granular control as their needs matured. The design job was sequencing: what to decide for the customer, what to ask, and when to reveal the deeper settings so the product felt simple at first and powerful later.
