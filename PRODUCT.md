# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Founders and product leaders at AI companies whose product works technically but isn't landing with users. The demo is real and the engineering ships, but new users can't tell what the AI is for, hesitate at the moments that matter, misread what it will and won't do, and quietly leave. Adoption lags the demo. Support fields the same questions the product should answer.

They're evaluating a senior designer who has done this exact work, not a junior, not an agency, not a generalist freelancer. The question they're holding is: does this person have the judgement to fix it, and is the next step small enough that I can try them?

## Product Purpose

Simple Bit Design is Craig Dennis's consulting studio for AI products. The site exists to position that judgement clearly enough that the right founders self-identify and book the $4k AI Product Teardown: a one-to-two-week diagnosis-plus-redesign that serves as a low-commitment first step toward larger work.

Success is the *right* prospects booking, not the most prospects. People who recognise their problem in the copy, trust the work in the case studies, and want a senior designer's judgement rather than a deck of generic recommendations.

## Brand Personality

Expert, plainspoken, warm.

**Expert.** Every word is calibrated. The studio speaks like someone who has shipped the exact thing the reader is wrestling with, because Craig has (Head of Product Design at Smith.ai, an AI voice-receptionist company).

**Plainspoken.** No consulting jargon, no design-language theatre, no portfolio-poetry. Senior readers, plain words. "AI got you to 70%. I bring the 30%" lands because it doesn't hide behind abstraction.

**Warm.** Cream, panel, teal, orange. Editorial, not corporate. The reader should feel they're being addressed by a person, not a brand system.

## Anti-references

The site explicitly should NOT look like:

- **Generic SaaS landing.** Centered hero, gradient text, icon-and-stat card grid, "trusted by" logo wall, the default Vercel-template look. The category reflex for tools.
- **"AI startup" aesthetic.** Neon-on-black, terminal vibe, dark-mode-by-default, glowing-orb illustrations. The category reflex when the topic is AI, which it is, so this is the trap one tier deeper.
- **Agency template.** Parallax everything, glassmorphism cards, oversized motion, scroll-jacking, "creative studio" bravado. The pretence-of-craft look.
- **Sober fintech / consultancy.** Navy + gold, stock photography of handshakes, corporate distance, "Trusted by Fortune 500" framing. The trust-by-suit reflex.

If any of these are recognisable in the output, the design has failed.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** The site IS the demo of Craig's judgement. Every choice in type, color, copy, density, and restraint is something he would defend in a teardown of someone else's product. No "do as I say" moments.

2. **Reframe over restate.** The case studies follow a pattern: "It looked like an X problem. It was actually a Y problem." That move, the strategic reframe, is the product. The site should sell the reframe, not just the deliverable.

3. **Plainspoken, never performative.** Senior readers, plain words. No consulting jargon, no fashion-y design talk, no portfolio-poetry. Trust is earned by being legible, not by sounding clever.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA across the site: contrast, focus visibility, semantic markup, keyboard navigation. The existing a11y test suite enforces this and should stay green.

Beyond AA: honour `prefers-reduced-motion`. Any motion the site adds must have a reduced-motion fallback that preserves meaning without the animation. The site should never punish someone who has expressed they don't want motion.
